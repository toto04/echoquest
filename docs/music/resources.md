# Resources: annotated reading list

A curated selection from the research notes. Each link was retrieved by a researcher unless marked *(not opened)*. The full source lists, with confidence flags, are in [`research_notes/Music composition guidelines/`](../../research_notes/Music%20composition%20guidelines/) and [`research_notes/Audio rhythm game music design/`](../../research_notes/Audio%20rhythm%20game%20music%20design/).

## Start here (the five most useful)

1. [Open Music Theory 2e: phrase archetypes (sentence, period)](https://human.libretexts.org/Bookshelves/Music/Music_Theory/Open_Music_Theory_2e_(Gotham_et_al.)/03%3A_Form/3.02%3A_The_Phrase_Archetypes_and_Unique_Forms): free textbook. The phrase shapes in [02](02-calls.md) come from here.
2. [Schellenberg et al. 2002, *Expectancy in melody: tests of children and adults*](https://sites.utm.utoronto.ca/sites/sites.utm.utoronto.ca.glenn_website/files/download/JEPGeneral.pdf): what 5- to 11-year-olds expect the next note to be. The basis of the "step first, leap later" rule.
3. [Winifred Phillips: pure vertical layering (GDC 2021 series)](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/blogs/pure-vertical-layering-for-game-music-composers-from-spyder-to-sackboy-gdc-2021-), with the [hybrid horizontal/vertical part](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/game-platforms/hybrid-horizontal-vertical-structure-for-game-music-composers-from-spyder-to-sackboy-gdc-2021-): how to write layers that work alone and together.
4. [Peter and the Wolf: Boosey & Hawkes introduction](https://www.boosey.com/downloads/peterwolf/introduction.pdf) and the [St. Louis Symphony "Musical Characters" lesson](https://slso.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/12/peter-wolf-musical-characters.pdf): instrument = character, taught to 6–11-year-olds.
5. [ChatMusician (2024)](https://arxiv.org/html/2402.16153v1): how LLMs handle ABC notation and where they fail (bar arithmetic).

## Melody, motif and memorability

- [Pearce & Müllensiefen, review of Huron's *Sweet Anticipation*](https://www.marcus-pearce.com/assets/papers/huron06-review.pdf): ITPRA and the prediction effect in a few pages.
- [von Hippel 2002, expectations as learned heuristics](https://lbj.utexas.edu/sites/default/files/file/faculty/Expectations%20as%20learned%20heuristics.pdf): skip reversal versus regression to the mean.
- [Open Music Theory v1: classical theme functions](https://hybrid-pedagogy.github.io/openmusictheory/themeFunctions.html): one-line definitions of fragmentation, liquidation and sequence.
- [Hutchinson, *Music Theory for the 21st-Century Classroom*: Melodic Alteration](https://musictheory.pugetsound.edu/mt21c/MelodicAlteration.html): inversion, augmentation, retrograde, extension.
- [Berklee PULSE: exploring form with pentatonic scales (PDF)](https://pulse.berklee.edu/content/public/pub_unit_assets/lessons/lesson2_banded/Exploring%20Form.pdf): rhythm-first, restricted-pitch composing. The method behind [02](02-calls.md).
- [Van Balen et al. 2015, hook discovery (ISMIR)](https://archives.ismir.net/ismir2015/paper/000148.pdf): what makes song sections recognisable (repetition, typicality, a prominent lead).
- [Janssen, Burgoyne & Honing 2017, folk-song memorability](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2017.00621/full): short, expected, early, repeated phrases survive.
- [Margulis, "Why repetition can turn almost anything into music" (Aeon)](https://aeon.co/essays/why-repetition-can-turn-almost-anything-into-music): a short read on *On Repeat*.
- [Toussaint, Euclidean rhythms (2005)](https://cgm.cs.mcgill.ca/~godfried/publications/banff.pdf): the tresillo and other maximally even rhythms, and a generator for backing timelines.

## Children, pedagogy and emotion

- [Sato et al. 2019, children's versus adults' songs](https://0110.be/files/attachments/471/FMA2019_paper_13.pdf): range and scale-degree counts.
- [Sarrazin, *Music and the Child*, ch. 4 (SUNY open textbook)](https://courses.lumenlearning.com/suny-music-and-the-child/chapter/chapter-4-approaches-to-music-education-2/): Orff and Kodály practice in brief.
- [Boler, types of borduns](https://victoria-boler-857o.squarespace.com/blog/types-of-borduns-elementary-general-music): bordun patterns (a teacher blog; verify against AOSA).
- [Kodály Center American Folk Song Collection](https://kodalycollection.org/song.cfm?id=481): searchable by tone set. Try *l, d, m, s*.
- [Dynamic Music Room: Kodály counting syllables](https://dynamicmusicroom.com/kodaly-counting-system/): ta, ti-ti, syn-co-pa, tam-ti.
- [Hunter, Schellenberg & Stalinski 2011](https://sites.utm.utoronto.ca/sites/sites.utm.utoronto.ca.glenn_website/files/download/HunterEtAl2011JECP.pdf): emotion recognition in music by age, and children's preference for high-arousal music.
- [Frontiers 2024: development of auditory scene analysis](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/human-neuroscience/articles/10.3389/fnhum.2024.1352247/full): why a call over backing is harder for children.
- [Leibold & Bonino 2009](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2736737): repetition helps children pick out a target far more than it helps adults.

## Game music craft

- [Koji Kondo at GDC: interactive musical landscapes](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/console/gdc-koji-kondo-s-interactive-musical-landscapes): rhythm, balance, interactivity; phrase pools.
- [Michael Sweet on interactive music (Berklee)](https://online.berklee.edu/takenote/?p=14985): stingers, loops, re-sequencing in a page.
- [Rhythm Quest devlog 13](https://rhythmquestgame.com/devlog/13.html): call-and-response gaps as look-ahead time; density reads as difficulty.
- [Musica Mundana: battle themes and victory fanfares](https://www.chaoticblue.com/blog/2013/05/musica-mundana-on-battle-themes-and-victory-fanfares/): what normal, boss and victory music do.
- [Game Music Theory: the Mario cadence](https://gamemusictheory.blogspot.com/2011/04/mario-cadence.html) and [Game Music Analysis Club: the FF victory fanfare](https://gamemusicanalysisclub.substack.com/p/3-victory-theme-final-fantasy-nes).
- [Splice: Pokémon's I–♭VII–I](https://splice.com/blog/pokemon-most-important-chord-progression/): Mixolydian as adventure.
- [Murphy, leitmotif functions (DiGRA 2019)](https://dl.digra.org/index.php/dl/article/download/1820/1820): nine functions of a leitmotif, with JRPG examples.
- [Smith, the semiotics of glissandi in games (MTO 2026)](https://www.mtosmt.org/issues/mto.26.32.1/mto.26.32.1.smith.pdf): comic versus horror glides. Sets the "never scary" palette.
- [Tufts Now on Frank Lehman's "sound of evil"](https://now.tufts.edu/2018/01/12/professor-turns-dark-side): the minor-third villain progression.
- [Game Developer: making boss music](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/audio/getting-making-game-music-that-fits-classic-genre-series-boss-music): pacing by fight length, phases.
- [Designing Sound: loudness in game audio](https://designingsound.org/2013/02/loudness-in-game-audio/): where the −23/−18 LUFS conventions come from.

## AI composition, formats and evaluation

- [ByteComposer](https://arxiv.org/html/2402.17785v1), [ComposerX](https://arxiv.org/html/2404.18081v1), [CoComposer](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.00132v1): plan-first and multi-agent composition; single agent plus tools is competitive.
- [Bhandari & Colton, structure in generative music (review)](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2403.07995): why form-first helps.
- [MusPy metrics](https://hermandong.com/muspy/metrics.html): exact definitions of pitch-in-scale, groove consistency, empty-beat rate.
- [Hennig et al. 2011](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3202537): correlated, not random, humanisation.

## 1. Which text formats LLMs handle best

### Takeaway
ABC notation is the format the literature uses most for LLM composition. It is about 2.6 times more compact than MIDI-event or REMI token streams and writes repetition out explicitly. General-purpose models produce *parseable* ABC most of the time but get *bar durations* wrong often. No paper compares JSON events, step grids or Strudel mini-notation head to head for general LLMs. For EchoQuest's 4-pitch calls, a fixed-width step grid makes bar-sum errors impossible by construction, and ABC (validated) fits the freer backing tracks.

### Cited Findings
- ChatMusician (Yuan et al., 2024) chose ABC because it is compact and "intrinsically encodes musical repetition and structure". Average tokens per song over 1,000 songs: ABC 288.21, REMI 753.41, MIDI-like 728.60, EnCodec audio 12,577.46, so ABC is "around 38% of MIDI-based representations". The authors also say MIDI's performance detail "can lead to quantization errors and unstable rhythms when being tokenized". — [ChatMusician, arXiv 2402.16153](https://arxiv.org/html/2402.16153v1)
- Parse success on 500 prompts (GPT models told "Please respond in ABC notation."): ChatMusician 99.6%, GPT-4 94.6%, GPT-3.5 65.4%. GPT-4's phrase-level repetition rate (repeat signs present) was 70.2%. GPT-4 scored low on the form-and-motif task because "most samples generated by GPT-4 of this task contain malformed ABC notation". — [ChatMusician](https://arxiv.org/html/2402.16153v1)
- MuPT argues LLMs are "inherently more compatible with ABC Notation" than MIDI. It introduces Synchronized Multi-Track ABC (SMT-ABC) because multi-track generation produced "misaligned measures from different tracks". — [MuPT, arXiv 2404.06393](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.06393)
- NotaGen uses *interleaved* ABC: all voices of one bar are put on one line with `[V:]` markers so durations stay aligned across voices, and full-rest bars are dropped (length cut to 80.7% on average). — [NotaGen, arXiv 2502.18008](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.18008v4)
- SongComposer represents a melody as tuples of pitch (MIDI 48 to 83) plus integer duration and rest in 1/16-beat units, separated by `|`, "inspired by the beat-based REMI representation". GPT-4 used few-shot as a baseline scored lower on pitch and duration distribution similarity (36.43 vs 50.75; 42.94 vs 57.71). — [SongComposer, arXiv 2402.17645](https://arxiv.org/html/2402.17645v2)
- REMI (Pop Music Transformer) imposes "a metrical structure in the input data" (bar and position events) so models can better learn the beat-bar-phrase hierarchy than with MIDI-like note-on/off/time-shift events. — [Huang & Yang 2020, arXiv 2002.00212](https://arxiv.org/abs/2002.00212)
- LilyPond benchmark (four open 14 to 22B code models): zero-shot compile rates 48.6% to 79.3%. Few-shot with corpus examples *dropped* compile rates to 19.9% to 45.2%. Hand-written simple examples raised them to 97.1% to 99.9%. Bar counting was nearly impossible (0 to 3% exact), and models defaulted to typical phrase lengths (16, 24, 32, 40). — [Spanio et al. 2026, arXiv 2606.08722](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.08722v1)
- ABC-Eval (2025): bar-count estimation ranged from 30% (DeepSeek-chat) to 99% (GPT-5-mini). Next-bar prediction topped out at 50.42% (GPT-5), and error detection at macro-F1 27.22. Simple-task performance is "highly sensitive to prompt variations". — [ABC-Eval, arXiv 2509.23350](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.23350v1)
- The only format studied by Kumar et al. was ABC. Their reason: "small local errors can invalidate entire sequences". — [Kumar et al. 2026, arXiv 2601.22764](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.22764v1)
- Strudel mini-notation packs events into a "cycle": space-separated steps share one cycle, `[]` subdivides, `<>` alternates per cycle, `~` or `-` is a rest, `@` elongates, `!` replicates, `,` stacks a chord, and `(3,8)` is Euclidean. Adding events shortens each one rather than lengthening the pattern. — [Strudel mini-notation docs](https://strudel.cc/learn/mini-notation/)
- A search for a direct GPT-4/Claude comparison of ABC vs MIDI-JSON vs piano roll found none. Several systems ask the LLM for JSON MIDI data but report no comparison against ABC. — (search extract only) [Santa Clara Univ. senior project](https://scholarcommons.scu.edu/context/elec_senior/article/1107/viewcontent/Music_Creation_and_Recommendation_Applications_Publicaton.pdf)

### Inferences
- **Calls (melody, 4 pitches): use a fixed-width step grid, not ABC.** One string per bar with exactly `steps_per_bar` tokens from a closed vocabulary: `D F A C` (onset of that pitch), `-` (hold the previous note), `.` (rest). Example in 4/4 with eighth-note steps: `"D . F . A - - ."`. A bar is valid if and only if `len(tokens) == 8`, so the meter error that ABC invites cannot occur. Pitch errors cannot occur either, because no other symbol is legal. Contour (Parsons code) and rhythm can be read straight off the string. This is Strudel-like (one token per step) but with a fixed step count, so a bar never silently changes resolution.
- **Backing tracks: ABC (one voice per line, interleaved per bar as in NotaGen) or the same grid per drum lane.** ABC suits bass lines and chord patterns because it is what models have seen most, and `|: :|` and `%%MIDI` directives make repetition and instrument choice explicit. Drums fit a step-sequencer grid better (`kick: "x . . . x . . ."`). Always parse with a tool and check bar sums (section 2).
- **Avoid absolute-time JSON event lists** (`{"t": 3.75, "d": 0.25, "p": 69}`) as the format the LLM *writes*. Every event then needs arithmetic, the exact weakness the bar-sum results show. Use JSON only as the machine-generated intermediate between grid/ABC and MIDI.
- **Code as music is a strong option for coding agents.** Ask the agent for a short Python script (pretty_midi/mido) that builds the backing from named loops and functions (`for bar in range(8): add_bass(bar, chord[bar])`). Repetition then becomes structural rather than copied text. This is untested in the literature retrieved here; treat it as an experiment.
- LilyPond and MusicXML are poor choices as the authoring format: LilyPond compiles unreliably for open models, and MusicXML is very verbose (no source was retrieved on MusicXML token cost). REMI tokens and MIDI-like tokens are formats for training models, not for prompting one.

### Gaps
- No controlled comparison of text formats for Claude or GPT-5-class models on short, constrained snippets was found. A small in-house A/B (same brief, grid vs ABC vs JSON, 20 samples each, scored by the validators in section 4) would settle it for EchoQuest.
- ABC parse rates are from GPT-4-era models. ABC-Eval suggests newer reasoning models count bars far better, but generation-side bar-sum error rates for current models were not found.

## 2. Failure modes of LLM-written music, and mitigations

### Takeaway
The documented failures are malformed syntax, bars that do not sum to the time signature (by far the most common with GPT-4: 68.8% time-signature error), notes outside instrument range, misaligned voices, missing cadences, shallow structure and too little development. Every system that works well adds a check-and-repair stage: a reviewer agent, a revision agent restricted to "minimum intervention", music21-based tool checks, or generate-many-then-select. Multi-agent pipelines help more with reliability and length than with musical quality.

### Cited Findings
- ByteComposer measured GPT-4's ABC output: time-signature error 68.8%, instrument-range error 48.8%, completeness 56.3%. GPT-4 can "excel in process description but fall short in generation quality". Its pipeline is conception analysis, then draft, then self-evaluation and modification ("objective errors are identified based on music theory and subsequently corrected"), then aesthetic selection by a reward model among error-free candidates. The Eval Tools-box uses music21 and a custom ABC parser. — [ByteComposer, arXiv 2402.17785](https://arxiv.org/html/2402.17785v1)
- ComposerX (GPT-4-Turbo, AutoGen group chat, at most 12 rounds) uses Leader, Melody, Harmony, Instrument, Reviewer and Arrangement agents. The Reviewer critiques melodic structure, harmony and counterpoint, rhythmic complexity, instrumentation, and form. Revision runs Melody, then Harmony, then Instrument. Generation success was 98.2% (GPT-4-Turbo) vs 73.0% (GPT-3.5-Turbo). Multi-agent output was about 3 times longer (ABC length about 1006 vs 338 to 366 characters for single-agent). 32.2% of its pieces passed as human, against 55.4% for real human music. — [ComposerX, arXiv 2404.18081](https://arxiv.org/html/2404.18081v1)
- ComposerX's melody guidance to agents: clear phrase divisions, phrases of no more than two consecutive measures, phrase endings on a stable chord tone, steps and thirds preferred over leaps, varied rhythm. It also uses a chain of thought (song info, then a 16-bar chord progression, then a 16-bar melody) and ABC templates per agent. — [ComposerX](https://arxiv.org/html/2404.18081v1)
- CoComposer lists ComposerX's weaknesses: only an 18.4% "good case rate", notes outside instrument range, "difficulty in multi-voice alignment", and "inadequate cadence resolution" (abrupt endings). Its Revision Agent checks that each measure's duration matches `M:` and fixes only confirmed errors ("minimum intervention principle"). A single GPT-4o agent scored about the same as the five-agent system on enjoyment and usefulness (CE 6.72 vs 6.75, CU 7.77 vs 7.76); the multi-agent gain was mainly production complexity (4.13 vs 3.92). Only 20 prompts were used, with no significance testing. — [CoComposer, arXiv 2509.00132](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.00132v1)
- The structure review finds that systems now handle motifs and repetition, but "modelling nuanced development and variation of themes" is still hard. Theme Transformer repeats themes but struggles with "creating variations and evolving the recurring themes". — [Bhandari & Colton 2024, arXiv 2403.07995](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2403.07995)
- In the LilyPond benchmark, models were almost unable to *detect* errors in scores (best macro-F1 about 0.07). Self-critique by the same model is therefore weak at spotting syntax or bar errors. — [Spanio et al. 2026](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.08722v1)
- Kumar et al. note that "small local errors can invalidate entire sequences" of ABC. — [arXiv 2601.22764](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.22764v1)

### Inferences
- **Never trust the model to check its own bars or pitches; use code.** The pipeline should be: generate, parse, check deterministically, return a machine-readable error list to the agent ("bar 3 has 9 steps, expected 8"; "note E4 not allowed in call track"), and let it repair with minimum intervention. Cap repair loops at about 3, then regenerate from scratch.
- **Generate N, filter, then rank.** Ask for 5 to 10 candidates per brief, drop the invalid ones, score the rest with the metrics in section 4, and give the top 2 or 3 to a human for listening. This copies ByteComposer's "aesthetic selection" without a trained reward model.
- **One agent with tools beats a committee of chat agents for 1 to 8 bar snippets.** CoComposer's single-agent baseline was about as good as the five-agent version. For EchoQuest, use one composer agent plus a deterministic validator and an optional critic prompt that receives the *metrics report*, not the raw notes.
- Failure modes to expect in EchoQuest calls, with the guard for each:
  - wrong length: grid width check;
  - illegal pitch: closed vocabulary;
  - aimless melody with no repetition: require a repeated cell (section 3);
  - every step filled with notes: density cap;
  - all equal durations: require at least 2 distinct note lengths, or a rest, per call;
  - calls in one encounter too similar: Parsons/rhythm distance check;
  - no clear ending: last onset on a strong beat, or the final note at least as long as the longest earlier note.
  - Backing: notes outside the scale or chord, a bass in the melody's register, loops that are not exactly N bars long.

### Gaps
- No study measured how often current Claude models violate bar sums in ABC; the 68.8% figure is GPT-4 (early 2024).
- No retrieved paper evaluates LLM-composed music for children or for memorability, which is EchoQuest's real quality criterion.

## 3. Structure-first prompting (form, then motif, then development)

### Takeaway
The literature supports planning before writing. Successful LLM pipelines decompose the task (concept or attributes, then harmony or form, then melody), and the symbolic-generation field calls this "sub-task decomposition": a structural plan first, then content. Development of themes is still the weakest point, so it should be specified with named, checkable operations rather than left to the model.

### Cited Findings
- Sub-task decomposition splits generation into "smaller, more manageable steps (typically 2 stages)": a structural plan, then content; "The plan ensures overall coherence". Examples: MeloForm (rule-based melody for a given form such as verse-chorus or rondo, then neural refinement), PopMNet (a bar-relation graph, then melody), WuYun (a melodic skeleton, then the full melody), Compose & Embellish (a lead sheet, then the performance). — [Bhandari & Colton 2024](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2403.07995)
- The same review recommends explicit compositional techniques: "fragmentation, inversion, augmentation, diminution, stretto, sequence, and modulation". — [Bhandari & Colton 2024](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2403.07995)
- ComposerX's chain of thought is song information, then the chord progression, then the melody. — [ComposerX](https://arxiv.org/html/2404.18081v1)
- ByteComposer's first stage maps the brief onto musical attributes (instrument, mode, rhythm, tempo, structure) before any notes are drafted. — [ByteComposer](https://arxiv.org/html/2402.17785v1)
- ChatMusician's benchmark tasks condition generation on given chords, melodies, motifs and musical forms, and it beat GPT-4 on following them. — [ChatMusician](https://arxiv.org/html/2402.16153v1)

### Inferences
- A prompt scaffold for an EchoQuest call, one step per message or one section per response, each machine-checkable:
  1. **Brief**: purpose (enemy attack call, victory fanfare), mood, difficulty tier, tempo, meter, bars.
  2. **Form**: phrase letters per half-bar or bar, e.g. `a a b a'` (4 bars) or `x x y` (2 bars + answer), plus cadence points and where the rest for the child's echo falls.
  3. **Motif**: one cell given as a rhythm (`x . x x`) plus a Parsons contour (`* u u d`) and a starting pitch.
  4. **Development**: each later phrase named as an operation on the motif from a closed list valid for 4 fixed pitches: `repeat`, `sequence` (shift by one step along D F A C), `invert-contour`, `retrograde`, `augment` (double durations), `diminish`, `truncate` (fragment), `extend-ending`. Free modulation is not available, because the call pitches are fixed.
  5. **Realise**: only now write the grid. The validator then checks that each declared operation was really applied (e.g. bar 2 equals bar 1 for `repeat`), which also gives a repetition metric for free.
- Backing loops: chord loop first (e.g. 2 or 4 bars, chords chosen so D F A C stay consonant), then the bass rhythm, then drums, then the countermelody. Each layer is validated against the previous one (bass root equals chord root on beat 1).
- Keep a small library of hand-approved snippets as few-shot examples, written in exactly the target format. The LilyPond result (hand-written simple examples raised compile rates to about 98%, corpus examples lowered them) suggests short clean examples work better than large realistic ones.

### Gaps
- No paper tests structure-first prompting on general chat models for very short game cues. The evidence comes from long-form pieces and from trained models.

## 4. Objective metrics usable as automatic checks

### Takeaway
The standard symbolic metrics (MusPy, MuseGAN, Yang & Lerch's mgeval) are simple counts and histograms with exact published formulas. They were designed to compare *datasets*, but most work as per-snippet pass/fail gates or ranking scores. For EchoQuest the most useful are: pitch-in-scale rate and scale consistency (backing), used pitch classes, empty-beat rate, groove consistency (loops), note-length histogram and density (calls), average pitch interval, and Parsons-code distinctness between calls.

### Cited Findings
MusPy metric definitions (quoted from the docs):
- `pitch_in_scale_rate = #(notes_in_scale) / #(notes)` for a given root and mode, from MuseGAN. `scale_consistency = max_{root, mode} pitch_in_scale_rate(root, mode)`, from C-RNN-GAN. — [MusPy metrics](https://hermandong.com/muspy/metrics.html)
- `pitch_class_entropy = -sum_{i=0}^{11} P(pitch_class=i) log2 P(pitch_class=i)` (Jazz Transformer); `pitch_entropy` is the same over the 128 pitches. — [MusPy metrics](https://hermandong.com/muspy/metrics.html)
- `empty_beat_rate = #(empty_beats) / #(beats)`; `empty_measure_rate = #(empty_measures) / #(measures)` (MuseGAN). — [MusPy metrics](https://hermandong.com/muspy/metrics.html)
- `groove_consistency = 1 - 1/(T-1) * sum_{i=1}^{T-1} d(G_i, G_{i+1})`, where G_i is the binary onset vector of measure i and d is the Hamming distance (Wu & Yang, Jazz Transformer, ISMIR 2020). — [MusPy metrics](https://hermandong.com/muspy/metrics.html)
- `polyphony_rate = #(time_steps_where_multiple_pitches_are_on) / #(time_steps)` (threshold 2); `drum_in_pattern_rate = #(drum_notes_in_pattern) / #(drum_notes)` for duple or triple meter; also `pitch_range`, `n_pitches_used`, `n_pitch_classes_used`. — [MusPy metrics](https://hermandong.com/muspy/metrics.html)
- MuseGAN metrics: EB, "ratio of empty bars (in %)"; UPC, "number of used pitch classes per bar (from 0 to 12)"; QN, ratio of "qualified" notes, i.e. at least 3 time steps (about a 32nd note), which "shows if the music is overly fragmented"; DP, "ratio of notes in 8- or 16-beat patterns"; TD, tonal distance between a pair of tracks, where "Larger TD implies weaker inter-track harmonic relations". — [Dong et al., MuseGAN, arXiv 1709.06298](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1709.06298)
- pypianoroll implements qualified-note rate with a default of 2 time steps, which differs from the paper's 3. — (search extract only) [pypianoroll metrics](https://pypianoroll.readthedocs.io/en/latest/metrics.html)
- mgeval (Yang & Lerch) features: pitch count, pitch-class histogram, 12x12 pitch-class transition matrix, pitch range, average pitch interval (semitones between consecutive notes), note count, average inter-onset interval, note-length histogram (12 classes, 24 with rests, basic unit bar/96), and note-length transition matrix. — [mgeval GitHub](https://github.com/RichardYang40148/mgeval)
- Yang & Lerch's evaluation compares intra-set and inter-set distance distributions per feature (exhaustive pairwise cross-validation, smoothed into PDFs) using KL divergence and overlapped area. — [MGEval blog post](https://richardyang40148.github.io/TheBlog/post_mgeval.html). Published in *Neural Computing and Applications* (online 2018). — (search extract only) [Georgia Tech listing](https://musicinformatics.gatech.edu/?p=422)
- The mgeval repository has no licence file (GitHub reports no licence). — [GitHub API, RichardYang40148/mgeval](https://github.com/RichardYang40148/mgeval)
- pretty_midi exposes `get_pitch_class_histogram(use_duration=...)`, `get_pitch_class_transition_matrix`, `get_piano_roll(fs=100)`, `get_beats`, `get_downbeats`, `get_onsets`. — [pretty_midi source](https://github.com/craffel/pretty-midi/blob/main/pretty_midi/pretty_midi.py)
- music21 key finding offers Krumhansl-Schmuckler-style weightings (KrumhanslSchmuckler, AardenEssen, BellmanBudge, SimpleWeights, TemperleyKostkaPayne). `analyze('key')` defaults to AardenEssen and stores alternatives in `Key.alternateInterpretations`. — [music21 analysis.discrete](https://www.music21.org/music21docs/moduleReference/moduleAnalysisDiscrete.html)
- Parsons code: `*` for the first note, then `u`/`d`/`r` for up, down, repeat relative to the previous note (e.g. Ode to Joy `* R U U R D D D D R U U R D R`). — [Parsons code, Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parsons_code)
- The LilyPond benchmark warns that a similarity metric (JS divergence) "is exploitable by narrow outputs". Read metrics side by side, not fused into one score. — [Spanio et al. 2026](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.08722v1)

### Inferences
Proposed gates for EchoQuest. The thresholds are my starting values, to be tuned against snippets the team likes:

| Check | Applies to | Definition | Starting gate |
|---|---|---|---|
| Format / bar sum | all | grid width = steps per bar; ABC bar durations = `M:` (music21) | must pass |
| Pitch vocabulary | calls | every pitch in {62, 65, 69, 72} | must pass |
| Pitch-in-scale rate | backing | MusPy, chosen root and mode (e.g. D Dorian) | ≥ 0.95 (chromatic passing notes rare) |
| Consonance with calls | backing | share of backing notes sounding with a call note that form m2/M7/tritone with it | ≤ 0.05 on strong beats |
| Register separation | backing | bass and pads outside D4 to C5, or quieter by ≥ 6 dB there | warn |
| Note count / density | calls | onsets per call; onsets per bar | e.g. tier 1: 2 to 4 notes; never more than 6 per bar at ≤ 100 BPM |
| Minimum IOI | calls | shortest gap between onsets in ms at the target tempo | ≥ 250 ms for young tiers |
| Rhythmic variety | calls | distinct note lengths, or a rest | ≥ 2 |
| Repetition | calls and loops | at least one bar or half-bar repeats exactly or as a declared transform; self-similarity matrix of bar onset vectors | ≥ 1 repeat in calls of 3 or more bars |
| Groove consistency | backing loops | MusPy formula over the loop's bars | ≥ 0.8 for drums/bass; sections may be lower |
| Empty-beat rate | backing | MusPy | ≤ 0.1 (backing should keep the pulse audible) |
| Average pitch interval / max leap | calls | mgeval PI; largest jump | warn if every move is a leap of a 5th or more (on D F A C the steps are thirds, so the whole set is leapy by nature) |
| Contour distinctness | sets of calls | Levenshtein distance between Parsons strings and between rhythm strings for every pair of calls in an encounter | sum ≥ 2 for every pair |
| Ending | calls | last onset on a strong beat, or longest note last; rest gap before the echo | must pass |
| Loop length | loops | MIDI end = N bars exactly; no notes start after bar N | must pass |

- Implement the checks in one `music_check.py` (reads the grid/ABC, writes MIDI, prints JSON results) so an agent can run it and read the errors. Use MusPy for the standard metrics. Reimplement mgeval features (a few lines each) rather than vendoring the code, because the repo has no licence.
- Key estimation is not very useful for calls (4 notes = Dm7, so key finders will say D minor or F major). Use it on backing only, as a sanity check that the intended mode wins.

### Gaps
- Yang & Lerch's paper (Springer) was not readable here, so the exact feature formulas come from the toolbox README and blog, not the paper.
- No validated thresholds exist for "catchy" or "memorable"; the gates above control quality, they do not prove it. Human listening (and later, children's reproduction accuracy in playtests) is the real metric.

## 5. Python tooling to write, parse and analyse

### Takeaway
All the core Python libraries are permissively licensed. pretty_midi for writing and analysing, mido for low-level events, music21 for ABC parsing, measure checks and key analysis, MusPy for metrics. MIDIUtil is a minimal beat-based writer. One pitfall is program numbering: pretty_midi uses 0 to 127 while GM tables print 1 to 128.

### Cited Findings
- Versions and licences on PyPI or GitHub (checked 11 Nov 2026): mido 1.3.3, MIT; pretty_midi 0.2.11.post0, MIT; MIDIUtil 1.2.1, MIT; music21 10.5.2, BSD-3-Clause, needs Python ≥ 3.11; MusPy 0.5.0, MIT; pyfluidsynth 1.4.0, MIT; miditok 3.1.0, MIT; symusic 0.6.0, MIT. — [PyPI JSON API](https://pypi.org/project/pretty_midi/) (each package page at pypi.org/project/<name>); [music21 repo](https://github.com/cuthbertLab/music21); [mido repo](https://github.com/mido/mido); [MusPy repo](https://github.com/salu133445/muspy)
- pretty_midi `Instrument(program, is_drum)`: "MIDI program number (instrument index), in `[0, 127]`", and `is_drum` means channel 9 (0-based). `PrettyMIDI(resolution=220, initial_tempo=120.)` are the defaults. `fluidsynth(fs=None, synthesizer=None, ...)` renders via pyfluidsynth at 44,100 Hz by default and, if no SoundFont is given, uses the bundled `TimGM6mb.sf2`. — [pretty_midi source](https://github.com/craffel/pretty-midi/blob/main/pretty_midi/pretty_midi.py); [instrument.py](https://github.com/craffel/pretty-midi/blob/main/pretty_midi/instrument.py)
- TimGM6mb.sf2 is GPL-2 (Tim Brechbill 2004, David Bolton 2010). — [Debian copyright, timgm6mb-soundfont](https://metadata.ftp-master.debian.org/changelogs/main/t/timgm6mb-soundfont/unstable_copyright)
- MIDIUtil: `MIDIFile(1)`, `addTempo(track, time, tempo)`, `addNote(track, channel, pitch, time, duration, volume)`, with time and duration "in beats". — [MIDIUtil docs](https://midiutil.readthedocs.io/en/1.2.1/); licence MIT-style text in [License.txt](https://github.com/MarkCWirt/MIDIUtil)
- abc2midi (abcMIDI package, maintained by Seymour Shlien) is GPL-2.0-or-later. Current versions are abc2midi 5.08 (19 Oct 2026) and midi2abc 3.64. — [sshlien/abcmidi](https://github.com/sshlien/abcmidi)
- abc2midi directives relevant here: `%%MIDI beat a b c n` sets velocities for the first note in a bar (a), other strong notes (b) and the rest (c); `!mf!` equals `%%MIDI beat 90 80 65 1`, `!f!` equals `105 95 80 1`. `%%MIDI beatstring fppmpmp` sets per-position stress. `%%MIDI trim x/y` shortens notes to add articulation gaps. `%%MIDI drum d2zdd 35 38 38 100 50 50` with `%%MIDI drumon` adds a drum pattern. `%%MIDI gchord fzczfzcz` sets the chord accompaniment pattern. — [abcguide.txt](https://github.com/sshlien/abcmidi/blob/master/doc/abcguide.txt)

### Inferences
- Pipeline libraries: **pretty_midi** to build MIDI from the grid/ABC and run histograms; **music21** only for ABC input and measure checks (`converter.parse(text, format='abc')`, then compare each `Measure`'s `duration.quarterLength` with its `barDuration`; verify these attribute names against the music21 version installed); **MusPy** for the metric functions (I believe it offers converters such as `muspy.from_pretty_midi`, but did not verify this in this session). Use abc2midi as an alternative ABC-to-MIDI converter when its `%%MIDI beat`/`drum`/`gchord` features are wanted. It is a GPL command-line tool used offline, so nothing GPL ships with the game.
- Always pass an explicit SoundFont to `pretty_midi.fluidsynth()` (or render with the CLI, section 6), so the GPL TimGM6mb bank never silently becomes the sound of a shipped asset.
- Keep a single `gm.py` constant table with **0-based** program numbers to stop off-by-one errors (section 11 gives both).

### Gaps
- music21's ABC parser coverage of `%%MIDI` directives was not checked. It probably ignores them, so instrument assignments should live in a side file (YAML) rather than inside the ABC.

## 6. Rendering: FluidSynth command line and free GM SoundFonts

### Takeaway
FluidSynth (LGPL-2.1, v2.6.2) renders MIDI to WAV offline with `-F`. GeneralUser GS (permissive custom licence, v2.0.3) is the recommended GM bank: 30.7 MB, explicitly tuned for FluidSynth. FluidR3_GM and MuseScore_General are MIT. Avoid TimGM6mb (GPL-2) and Arachno (personal use) for shipped assets.

## 8. Getting it into Unity with sample-accurate timing

### Takeaway
Unity's sample-accurate primitive is `AudioSource.PlayScheduled(dspTime)`. Pre-rendered WAV clips scheduled on the DSP clock are the simplest and most robust route. Since calls use only 4 pitches, a call can be *data* (the grid string) played at runtime by scheduling 4 pre-rendered one-shot samples per instrument, which keeps it MIDI-like with no synthesiser. Runtime SoundFont synthesis (MeltySynth, MIT) is possible through `OnAudioFilterRead`, but it costs audio-thread CPU, has untested GeneralUser compatibility, and buys nothing the one-shot approach does not. DryWetMIDI (MIT) is for MIDI file I/O and analysis, not for sample-accurate audio. Maestro MPTK is a commercial-asset alternative.

### Cited Findings
- `PlayScheduled` "Plays the AudioSource.clip at a specific time on the absolute time-line that AudioSettings.dspTime reads from". Schedule "slightly in the future (~100-200ms)". It is "the preferred way to stitch AudioClips in music players because it is independent of the frame rate". If the time is already past, playback starts as soon as possible, and the missed audio is not reproduced. The documented example alternates two AudioSources, schedules about 1 s ahead, and advances `nextEventTime += 60.0f / bpm * numBeatsPerSegment`. — [Unity Scripting API: AudioSource.PlayScheduled](https://docs.unity.com/en-us/engine/6000.0/script-reference/unityengine/audiosource/playscheduled)
- `AudioSettings.dspTime` is in seconds, "based on the actual number of samples the audio system processes and is therefore much more precise than" `Time.time`. dspTime does not advance while the game is paused. — [Unity Scripting API: AudioSettings.dspTime](https://docs.unity.com/en-us/engine/6000.0/script-reference/unityengine/audiosettings/dsptime)
- `OnAudioFilterRead(float[] data, int channels)` runs on the audio thread (most Unity API calls are not allowed). Data is interleaved floats in [-1, 1], called "every ~20ms depending on the sample rate and platform". The process time is shown in red if the filter starves the mixer. It "is not supported on the Web platform". — [Unity Scripting API: OnAudioFilterRead](https://docs.unity.com/en-us/engine/6000.0/script-reference/unityengine/monobehaviour/onaudiofilterread)
- AudioClip import: Decompress On Load is for "smaller compressed sounds"; Vorbis decompressed in memory uses about 10 times the memory of the compressed data. Compressed In Memory decompresses on the mixer thread. Streaming has about 200 KB overhead per clip. PCM is for "short sound effects"; Vorbis/MP3 are "best for medium length sound effects and music". If Preload Audio Data is off, call `AudioClip.LoadAudioData` first to avoid playback delays. — [Unity Manual: Audio Clip import settings](https://docs.unity.com/en-us/engine/6000.0/manual/audio/reference/class-audio-clip)
- **MeltySynth**: a SoundFont synthesiser in C#, .NET Standard 2.1 only, "suitable for both real-time and offline synthesis", standard MIDI file support, reverb and chorus, "loop extension support". It "does not provide thread safety", and it only produces PCM (you feed it to an audio driver yourself). It is "heavily inspired by" C# Synth and TinySoundFont. Licence MIT (copyrights: Alex Veltsistas 2014, Bernhard Schelling 2017-2018, Nobuaki Tanaka 2021). — [sinshu/meltysynth README and LICENSE.txt](https://github.com/sinshu/meltysynth)
- **DryWetMIDI** (MIT, v8.0.3, Dec 2025): read, write, play and record MIDI, high-level note objects, quantising, Pattern composition API. Unity Asset Store packages exist (full and "nativeless"). Native binaries are needed for MIDI devices and default playback. The default `HighPrecisionTickGenerator` is "supported for Windows and macOS only"; `RegularPrecisionTickGenerator` has about 16 ms precision on Windows. Playback can be ticked manually, e.g. from Unity's frame update. — [melanchall/drywetmidi](https://github.com/melanchall/drywetmidi); [Tick generator docs](https://melanchall.github.io/drywetmidi/articles/playback/Tick-generator.html); [Using in Unity](https://melanchall.github.io/drywetmidi/articles/dev/Using-in-Unity.html)
- **Maestro MPTK** (Unity Asset Store, free and Pro): "Play, generate and control MIDI music in Unity, with a complete synthesis engine and custom SoundFont support"; prefabs MidiFilePlayer, MidiStreamPlayer, MidiSpatializer, etc. — [paxstellar.fr](https://paxstellar.fr/). Its API docs ship an "AI Skills" catalogue in both editions; "Some workflows require Pro features". — [mptkapi.paxstellar.com](https://mptkapi.paxstellar.com/). The vendor's comparison lists the MIDI file writer, multiple-SoundFont import, playlists/looping and external MIDI loading as Pro-only. Free ships with GeneralUser_GS_SoftSynth v1.44 and caps global volume at 1 and speed at 0.1 to 10 by default. Pro is listed at $65 (v2.20.0) and ships with no SoundFont. — (search extract only) [paxstellar.fr](https://paxstellar.fr/); [Asset Store, MPTK Pro](https://assetstore.unity.com/packages/tools/audio/maestro-midi-player-tool-kit-pro-115331)
- **CSharpSynthForUnity** (HowdyChad): a port of CSharpSynth to Unity, MIT, last pushed Feb 2020. A 2023 fork (TriggerCoder/CSharpSynthForUnity2.0) declares no licence. — [GitHub HowdyChad/CSharpSynthForUnity](https://github.com/HowdyChad/CSharpSynthForUnity); [TriggerCoder/CSharpSynthForUnity2.0](https://github.com/TriggerCoder/CSharpSynthForUnity2.0)
- The project's existing note already covers DSP-clock scheduling, Bluetooth latency and calibration. — [rhythm_game_design.md §4](../Audio%20rhythm%20game%20music%20design/rhythm_game_design.md); [audio_games_accessibility.md §7](../Audio%20rhythm%20game%20music%20design/audio_games_accessibility.md)

### Inferences
- **Recommended runtime architecture:**
  1. **Calls** are stored as text in game data (the grid string plus tempo and instrument). At runtime a `CallPlayer` converts steps to `dspTime` offsets and schedules one-shot clips (one WAV per pitch per instrument: 4 pitches × a few instruments, rendered from the house SoundFont) on a pool of AudioSources. Each note needs its own source, because a new `PlayScheduled` on the same source replaces the pending one (standard Unity behaviour, verify in a test). The same grid also drives judging (expected onsets) and haptics, so audio and gameplay can never drift apart.
  2. **Backing loops, stingers and fanfares** are pre-rendered WAV stems scheduled with `PlayScheduled` on the same beat clock (section 9).
  3. **Runtime synthesis** (MeltySynth in `OnAudioFilterRead`) only if a later feature really needs procedurally changing backing (e.g. per-child tempo changes beyond what time-stretching handles). Before adopting it, run GeneralUser's [SoundFont Spec Implementation Test](https://github.com/mrbumpy409/SoundFont-Spec-Test) through MeltySynth, because GeneralUser rates its ancestor TinySoundFont "Poor".
- Use **DryWetMIDI** only in editor tooling if a C# MIDI importer is wanted (e.g. an editor script turning `.mid` into the grid or a ScriptableObject of note times). Its playback clock is a timer, not the DSP clock.
- **Maestro MPTK** is the "buy instead of build" option if the team later wants live MIDI playback with SoundFonts inside Unity. It is not open source; check the Asset Store EULA and test its timing against `dspTime` before relying on it for judged calls.
- Format: 48 kHz, 16- or 24-bit mono WAV for one-shots, stereo for backing. Import one-shots as PCM + Decompress On Load + Preload; import loops as Vorbis (quality high) + Decompress On Load (short loops) or Compressed In Memory (long ones). Check `AudioSettings.outputSampleRate` on target machines and render at that rate to avoid resampling. Desktop outputs are commonly 48 kHz, but I did not retrieve a source for that.
- All tempo maths in double precision from `dspTime`; never accumulate float frame times.

### Gaps
- No measurement was found of MeltySynth CPU cost inside `OnAudioFilterRead`, or of its compliance with GeneralUser GS.
- The MPTK Free/Pro split and price come from search extracts and may be out of date.
- Unity's documentation does not state per-platform output latency for the DSP buffer settings (already noted in the accessibility note).

## 9. Exporting seamless loops (tails, loop points, formats)

### Takeaway
A loop clip must be exactly N bars long *and* contain the tail of its own previous pass, or there is a click or a reverb drop at the seam. The standard trick is to render the loop twice in a row and keep only the second pass, or to fold the tail onto the start. In Unity a cleaner alternative uses `PlayScheduled` with clips longer than the loop period, so each pass's tail overlaps the next. Use WAV or Ogg Vorbis, never MP3, for loops.

### Cited Findings
- MP3 cannot loop gaplessly without special handling: frames are 1152 samples and must be full (padding at the end); LAME adds 576 samples of encoder delay; decoders add 529 samples (about 12 ms at 44.1 kHz) on each restart; frames depend on previous frames (MDCT overlap, bit reservoir). — [Compuphase, Gapless looping MP3](https://www.compuphase.com/mp3/mp3loops.htm)
- Ogg Vorbis stores timestamps per page, which gives exact durations and made it "especially popular for video games, where perfectly looping music tracks ... are essential"; WAV has no encoder delay. — (search extract only) [Vimeo engineering, gapless audio](https://medium.com/vimeo-engineering-blog/a-brief-history-of-gapless-audio-and-what-you-can-do-about-it-ea9e1c343215)
- The "second pass" technique: Reaper's render option plays the region twice and records only the second pass, so the reverb tail cut off at the end continues into the start. A common tip is to bounce twice the loop length and keep the second half. Wrapping the tail onto the start makes the *first* play-through start with a tail "out of nowhere"; one fix is to play an un-wrapped intro version once, then loop the wrapped one. — (search extract only) [gamedev.net: making a track loop](https://gamedev.net/forums/topic/662581-making-a-track-loop/); [Steinberg forum: Render second pass](https://forums.steinberg.net/t/render-second-pass-when-bouncing/661814)
- FluidSynth's `player.reset-synth` resets the synth at the end of a song unless turned off "for seamless looping". Fast render stops at the last MIDI event. — [fluidsettings.xml](https://github.com/FluidSynth/fluidsynth/blob/master/doc/fluidsettings.xml); [src/fluidsynth.c](https://github.com/FluidSynth/fluidsynth/blob/master/src/fluidsynth.c)
- The PlayScheduled example advances the next start by exactly `60 / bpm * beatsPerSegment`, independent of clip length. — [Unity PlayScheduled](https://docs.unity.com/en-us/engine/6000.0/script-reference/unityengine/audiosource/playscheduled)

### Inferences
- **Option A, overlap scheduling (preferred for EchoQuest).** Render each loop as N bars plus a tail (e.g. 2 s, ending in silence). Schedule pass k at `t0 + k * N * barSeconds` on alternating AudioSources. The tail of pass k plays under pass k+1, exactly like a live performance. No wrapping is needed, the first pass is naturally clean, and the stinger at the end of a battle can be scheduled on the next bar line while the last tail rings out.
- **Option B, self-contained loop clip (for `AudioSource.loop = true` or for sharing outside Unity).** Generate MIDI containing the loop twice (plus padding), render, then cut exactly `[N*barSamples, 2*N*barSamples)` from the WAV. The cut must be computed in samples from tempo and sample rate (`barSamples = sr * 60 / bpm * beatsPerBar`). Choose tempos where `barSamples` is an integer at 48 kHz (e.g. 96, 100, 120, 125, 144 BPM in 4/4), so the bar grid never drifts by fractional samples. Ship an "intro" (first-pass) clip as well if the first entry must be clean.
- In the generated MIDI, keep notes from crossing the loop boundary unless that is intended. The validator should flag any note-off after bar N in the *loop* section.
- Validate the rendered loop automatically: length in samples equals the expected value; peak ≤ -1 dBFS; the RMS of the first and last 10 ms matches within a few dB (option B); and no DC offset.
- Never import MP3 for loops; give Unity WAV and let it encode Vorbis.

### Gaps
- No official Unity statement on gapless Vorbis looping in Unity 6 was retrieved. Test one loop on each target platform.
- The "render twice, keep second" advice is from practitioner forums, not formal documentation, but it follows directly from how reverb tails work.

## 10. MIDI realism: accents, humanisation, articulation, and where they are forbidden

### Takeaway
Simple MIDI sounds much better with (1) velocity shaped by metric position, (2) articulation (shorter short notes, small gaps), and (3) small timing deviations that are *correlated* over time rather than white noise. For EchoQuest the call track is the reference the child copies, so it must be exactly on the grid with deterministic velocities. Humanisation belongs only on backing layers that do not carry the beat used for judging.

### Cited Findings
- abc2midi's built-in metric-accent model uses three velocity levels: first note of the bar, other strong beats, everything else. Its dynamic marks map to `!p!` 60/50/35, `!mf!` 90/80/65, `!f!` 105/95/80, `!ff!` 120/110/95. Custom stress patterns use `%%MIDI beatstring` (e.g. `fppmpmp` for 7/8). Its optional BarFly stress models (`-BF`) apply style-specific stress for recognised rhythms such as jigs. — [abcguide.txt](https://github.com/sshlien/abcmidi/blob/master/doc/abcguide.txt)
- The KTH performance rule system (Director Musices) models expressive performance with rules such as Duration Contrast, Punctuation, Phrase Arch, Double Duration, Inégales, Ensemble Swing, Final Ritardando, Melodic Charge and Harmonic Charge. — [KTH: Examples of rules for music performance](https://www.speech.kth.se/music/performance/performance_rules.html). Duration Contrast: musicians "play the short notes shorter and the long notes longer than nominally written". Rule strength k = 2.2 is "medium" and 4.4 "exaggerated". — [KTH: Duration contrast](https://www.speech.kth.se/music/performance/Texts/duration_contrast.htm)
- Hennig et al. (PLoS ONE 2011): human timing deviations are long-range correlated, while DAW humanisers are "essentially random number generators" producing uncorrelated noise. Listeners preferred 1/f-correlated deviations over white-noise deviations and even judged them "more precise". — [Hennig et al. 2011, PMC3202537](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3202537). The listening test used 39 choir singers. — (search extract only) [DOI 10.1371/journal.pone.0026457](https://dx.doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0026457)
- Craig Anderton argues that humanising is deliberate placement, not randomness: snare slightly behind the beat for weight, hi-hat or shaker slightly ahead to push, delaying successive tom-fill notes, nudging bass against kick to choose which leads. Randomisation "works well only if you want to imitate someone who has had too much to drink" (paraphrased by the fetch tool). — [Craig Anderton, Humanize MIDI parts with timing tweaks](https://craiganderton.org/humanize-midi-parts-with-timing-tweaks/)
- Practitioner settings: Logic's humanise at about ±10 ticks is described as "subtle life"; one humaniser preset uses -5/+3 ms timing and -2/+3 velocity. — (search extract only) [Splice blog](https://splice.com/blog/humanize-your-drums/); [Sonicstate on Marla Mini Humanizer](https://sonicstate.com/amped/2026/08/01/marla-mini-humanizer-drag-and-drop-midi-humanization-for-macos/)
- Timing discrimination in isochronous sequences was measured by Friberg & Sundberg (JASA 98(5), 1995) using adjustment of one tone in a six-tone sequence. — (search extract only) [JASA, doi 10.1121/1.413218](https://zendy.io/pdf-viewer/10.1121/1.413218). See also the project's latency-JND notes in [audio_games_accessibility.md §7](../Audio%20rhythm%20game%20music%20design/audio_games_accessibility.md).

### Inferences
- **Call track (copied by the child): no humanisation at all.** Onsets exactly on the grid, durations from a fixed articulation table, velocities from a deterministic accent table. Recommended: beat 1 = 100, other main beats = 88, off-beats = 76; final note +6. Use the same values for every call, so loudness never hints at timing differently from one call to the next. Any "feel" (swing) must be part of the grid definition and identical when the game judges.
- **Beat anchor (kick, click, bass on downbeats): quantised.** At most ±3 velocity variation, no timing offset. Children and the judge both rely on it.
- **Free backing layers (pads, countermelody, shakers, fills): humanisation allowed.**
  - Timing: deterministic offsets per role (hi-hat or shaker -5 to -10 ms, snare backbeat +5 to +10 ms) plus a slow random walk of ±5 ms. The walk should be correlated (each offset = 0.8 × previous + small noise), not white noise.
  - Velocity: accent table ±6 random.
  - Duration contrast: shorten notes of an eighth or less to about 50 to 70% of the step (light staccato); keep long notes at 95 to 100%; legato lines may overlap by 10 to 20 ms on a mono instrument patch.

  These are my starting values, not sourced figures.
- Percussive call instruments (marimba, xylophone, kalimba, glockenspiel, music box) decay by themselves, so note-off timing barely matters. Still use a fixed short duration (e.g. 90% of the step) so the release is consistent between renders.
- Articulation as data: each instrument in the side file gets `{staccato: 0.55, normal: 0.9, legato: 1.02}` multipliers, and the grid's `-` hold token extends a note so that the articulation is chosen by the format, not improvised by the LLM.
- Implement accents and humanisation in the *renderer* (Python), never by asking the LLM for velocities. The LLM writes only notes and structure, and the code applies the performance rules reproducibly from a seed.

### Gaps
- No numeric humanisation ranges from controlled studies were retrieved. The Hennig paper's figure values did not come through as text. The ranges above are practitioner-level guesses to be tuned by ear.
- No source was found on whether expressive timing in a model makes children's reproduction worse. The no-humanisation rule for calls is a design inference from the game's judging needs.

### Inferences
- Candidate call timbres with fast attack and clear pitch in the D4 to C5 register: Marimba (12), Xylophone (13), Kalimba (108), Glockenspiel (9, sounds high; check whether the SoundFont transposes it), Music Box (10), Celesta (8), Steel Drums (114), Pizzicato Strings (45), Orchestral Harp (46). Values are 0-based for code. Ocarina (79) and Flute (73) are softer-attack options for "magic" calls. Taiko (116), Timpani (47) and Tuba (58) fit battle backing, not calls.
- The same GM program (Ocarina, Kalimba, Choir Aahs, ...) can sound very different from one bank to the next. Audition each program in the house SoundFont before locking the instrument list, because GM fixes only names, not sounds (midi.org states that GM does not define acoustic characteristics, per the page fetched: [midi.org GM1](https://midi.org/general-midi-level-1)).
- Drums for battle loops: 36 kick, 38 snare, 42/46 hats, 49 crash on section starts, 75/76/77 claves and wood blocks as "child-friendly" ticks. Keep the drum lane vocabulary in the grid format limited to these few names (kick, snare, hat, open, crash, block_hi, block_lo, clap, tamb) mapped to keys in code.

- The official MIDI Association GM1 PDF is behind a sign-up wall and was not retrieved. Values are from two consistent secondary sources.

## 12. Recommended end-to-end pipeline for EchoQuest

### Takeaway
Composer agent → constrained text (grid for calls, ABC/grid for backing) → deterministic validator with metric report → repair or regenerate → Python renderer (accents, articulation, humanisation by rule) → FluidSynth + GeneralUser GS → WAV stems and one-shots → Unity: calls played from text by DSP-scheduled one-shots, backing stems scheduled on the same beat clock.

### Cited Findings
- Check-and-repair stages and selection among valid candidates are what made LLM composition reliable in ByteComposer, ComposerX and CoComposer (sections 2 and 3).
- DSP-clock scheduling with `PlayScheduled` is Unity's documented way to stitch clips sample-accurately (section 8).
- FluidSynth with GeneralUser GS is the combination its author rates "Excellent" (section 6).

### Inferences
1. **Spec file per snippet** (YAML), written by the agent, validated by schema:
   ```yaml
   id: duel_goblin_call_02
   role: call            # call | loop | stinger | fanfare
   meter: 4/4
   steps_per_bar: 8
   tempo: 96
   form: [a, a, b]        # per bar
   motif: {rhythm: "x . x . x - - .", contour: "* u u", start: D}
   development: {bar2: repeat, bar3: invert-contour}
   instrument: marimba
   bars:
     - "D . F . A - - ."
     - "D . F . A - - ."
     - "A . F . D - - -"
   ```
   Backing: `role: loop`, a `chords` line per bar, then `tracks: {bass: abc-or-grid, drums: {kick: "x...x...", ...}}`.
2. **`tools/music/check.py`** returns JSON: hard errors (format, bar sums, vocabulary, loop length, declared development really applied) and soft metrics (section 4 table). The agent loop: generate 5 to 10 candidates → keep those with no hard errors → rank by soft metrics → the top 3 go to humans.
3. **`tools/music/render.py`** turns the spec into MIDI with pretty_midi. It applies the accent table and articulation and, for allowed layers only, seeded humanisation. It pads the end and, for loops, writes the twice-through version. Then it calls FluidSynth (dry, 48 kHz, s24) per stem and checks length and peak.
4. **Outputs**: `Audio/Music/<id>/<stem>.wav` plus the spec copied into Unity as a TextAsset. A `CallPlayer` reads the grid at runtime and schedules one-shots `Audio/Music/oneshots/<instrument>_<D4|F4|A4|C5>.wav`; a `LoopPlayer` schedules stems by bar.
5. **Licences to record** in a notices file: GeneralUser GS licence v2.0 (or FluidR3_GM MIT), plus MeltySynth MIT or DryWetMIDI MIT if either is ever added. FluidSynth (LGPL), abc2midi (GPL) and Strudel (AGPL) are offline tools only and not shipped.
6. **Docs**: if adopted, add the workflow to `docs/development-workflow.md`, and add a line to `docs/roadmap.md` when the pipeline lands (per CLAUDE.md conventions).

### Gaps
- None of this has been prototyped. The first step is to build `check.py` and `render.py` for the call grid only, generate 20 calls with Claude, and have the team rate them blind against 5 hand-written calls.

## Recommended further reading

1. [ChatMusician (Yuan et al., 2024), arXiv 2402.16153](https://arxiv.org/html/2402.16153v1): why ABC suits LLMs (token counts), parse rates for GPT-4 vs a fine-tuned model, and repetition and form-following benchmarks.
2. [ByteComposer (Liang et al., 2024), arXiv 2402.17785](https://arxiv.org/html/2402.17785v1): a four-stage "human-like" agent pipeline (concept, draft, self-check with music21, selection) and the GPT-4 error rates (68.8% time-signature errors) that justify validators.
3. [ComposerX (Deng et al., 2024), arXiv 2404.18081](https://arxiv.org/html/2404.18081v1): multi-agent roles, reviewer criteria and concrete melody-writing rules in the prompts; good template material for a critic prompt.
4. [CoComposer (2025), arXiv 2509.00132](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.00132v1): a critique of ComposerX, a bar-duration Revision Agent with "minimum intervention", and evidence that a single agent is nearly as good.
5. [Bhandari & Colton, Motifs, Phrases, and Beyond (EvoMUSART 2024)](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2403.07995): a review of structure modelling; the case for plan-then-content decomposition and named development techniques.
6. [ABC-Eval (2025), arXiv 2509.23350](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.23350v1): current-model (GPT-5, Gemini 2.5, DeepSeek) abilities on ABC; strong on syntax, weak on segment-level reasoning and error detection.
7. [MusPy metrics documentation](https://hermandong.com/muspy/metrics.html): exact formulas for scale consistency, pitch-class entropy, empty-beat rate, groove consistency and drum-pattern metrics, ready to call from Python.
8. [mgeval toolbox (Yang & Lerch)](https://github.com/RichardYang40148/mgeval): the canonical feature set (PCH, PCTM, IOI, note-length histograms) and the intra/inter-set method. Note that it has no licence, so reimplement rather than copy.
9. [GeneralUser GS documentation](https://github.com/mrbumpy409/GeneralUser-GS/blob/main/documentation/README.md) and [licence](https://github.com/mrbumpy409/GeneralUser-GS/blob/main/documentation/LICENSE.txt): the synth compatibility table (which synths break it), the recommended FluidSynth effect settings, and the permissive licence text.
10. [FluidSynth man page](https://github.com/FluidSynth/fluidsynth/blob/master/doc/fluidsynth.1) and [settings reference](https://github.com/FluidSynth/fluidsynth/blob/master/doc/fluidsettings.xml): every render flag and default (gain 0.2, 44.1 kHz, file format, reset-synth for looping).
11. [Unity AudioSource.PlayScheduled](https://docs.unity.com/en-us/engine/6000.0/script-reference/unityengine/audiosource/playscheduled): the DSP-clock stitching pattern every EchoQuest music player should follow.
12. [MeltySynth](https://github.com/sinshu/meltysynth): the MIT C# SoundFont synth, the runtime-synthesis option if pre-rendering ever stops being enough.
13. [abc2midi guide (abcguide.txt)](https://github.com/sshlien/abcmidi/blob/master/doc/abcguide.txt): practical, documented velocity-accent (`%%MIDI beat`), articulation (`trim`), drum and chord-pattern directives, a ready-made "realism" layer for ABC.
14. [Hennig et al. 2011, The nature and perception of fluctuations in human musical rhythms](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3202537): why correlated rather than random humanisation sounds more human (and more precise).
15. [Compuphase: gapless looping MP3](https://www.compuphase.com/mp3/mp3loops.htm): exactly why MP3 loops click (frame padding, encoder and decoder delay). Read it once, then never ship MP3 loops.
